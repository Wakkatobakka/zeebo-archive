ZEEBO / PLATFORM HISTORY

Making games for Zeebo

The console, its development environment, and the games made for it.

A console with mobile roots

Zeebo Inc. was formed by Tectoy and Qualcomm. Its console used Qualcomm mobile technology and delivered games through a built-in 3G connection instead of discs. At GDC 2009, the company presented a system intended for households in developing markets.[1]

The intended audience

In May 2009, CEO John Rizzo discussed an initial Brazilian rollout, followed by Mexico. The executives described a mix of established games and new titles suited to local audiences. Qualcomm’s Mike Yuen explained their approach to finding that content:[1]

The development environment

The May 2009 Developer Guide describes Zeebo software version 1.0 as based on Qualcomm’s BREW 4.0.2 SDK, with OpenGL ES graphics and IHID controller extensions. Its specifications list VGA television output at 640 × 480 in a 4:3 aspect ratio.[2]

Tools and testing

The guide covers memory management, controller input, 3D graphics, audio, debugging and game submission. It includes instructions for BREW AppLoader and Logger, loading builds from an SD card, and tuning television output. Developers’ individual accounts describe how they used this environment for particular games.[2]

The surviving collection

OpenZeebo’s compilation notes are dated 23 October 2011. They map numeric application IDs to game and system-app names. The archive retains those IDs, the original package variants and the measured file fingerprints, alongside the game histories.[3]
