The game

Billy and Jimmy Lee set out to rescue Marian from Machine Gun Willy’s gang. The Zeebo remake expands the arcade game from four missions to six, with three new locations. Weapons dropped by enemies can be picked up and used, and oil drums can be thrown or kicked.[2]

Finishing the game opens up more playable fighters. The extra battle mode lets players return as the enemies, with eighteen unlockable characters and their own sets of moves.[2]

The controller supports both single-button moves and some combined-button commands. Weapons can be dropped deliberately, while dashing and launch attacks can be linked into mid-air combinations. Rockman’s detailed Zeebo review made those juggling attacks a particular attraction.[3]

Development

Brizo Interactive and Million are credited on this remake. Its Zeebo staff include main planner Hiroto Ikuta, main programmer Seiji Ando, graphic artists Hiroto Kittaka and Yuji Watanabe, and composer Hideki Asanaka. Souta Ichino led the project, with Ryosuke Jinno as sub-producer.[2][4]

Brizo later made an iPhone Double Dragon. In an April 2011 interview, Jinno described that release as the studio’s third Double Dragon project, following its earlier mobile and Zeebo work.[5]

Release and reception

Access itself became part of the game’s history. Double Dragon Dojo contributor Rockman described buying a Brazilian Zeebo through an auction site, using Google Translate, and finding one with Double Dragon already installed. He praised its fighting options, enemy roster and unlockable Extra mode.[3]

A review reproduced at Zeebo Club in March 2010 was more reserved about the fighting. It praised the redrawn sprites but described attack sequences that could not be cancelled, leaving a fighter exposed when punches missed. The writer considered the result enjoyable, with less fluid combat than it could have had.[6]
