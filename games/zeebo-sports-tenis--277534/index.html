The game

Tênis plays doubles matches with characters that move automatically. With the Boomerang, the player swings to hit the ball. Winning earns points towards unusual rackets, including a guitar, broom and frying pan.[2][3]

Development

Bruno Evangelista led programming and Seigi Sato led art. The credits name Hugo Faria as producer and André Nogueira as acting producer, with further programming, art and testing recorded in a separate 2010 additional-resources section.[4]

At SBGames 2009, Bruno Evangelista used Tênis to illustrate the studio’s progress: a contemporaneous report quotes him comparing 10,000 triangles per scene in Tênis with 6,000 in Rolimã. These were the lead programmer’s stated figures, rather than an independent benchmark.[5]

Release and reception

Tênis began as a Boomerang-only game on 15 October 2009. The August 2010 Zeebo Sports edition added Z-Pad play. Existing owners could request a free upgrade, which erased their local progress.[2][6]

J.Vila’s Zeebo Club review praised the eccentric cast and unlockable rackets, but noticed a slight delay between a swing and the game’s response. He found one court and no career mode restrictive; the daily online-ranking update helped sustain competition. He awarded 7.5/10. This review concerns the original Boomerang release.[7]
