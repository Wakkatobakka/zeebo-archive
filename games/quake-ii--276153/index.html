The game

After the Strogg attack Earth, humanity launches an assault on their homeworld. The operation fails, leaving a surviving soldier to fight through the aliens’ military installations and disable their war machine. Quake II builds its first-person shooting around this campaign through enemy territory.[2]

Development

Adapting Quake II meant bringing the original C game into an ARM/BREW environment. Producer Tiago Tex Pine recalls that the PC game’s memory-allocation approach did not fit the console’s operating system. Performance and rendering needed substantial attention rather than a straightforward recompile.[3]

Pine led a seven-person group of developers and QA staff. He tested and tuned the controls and camera for the Zeebo hardware, managed the project, and supplied some voices himself. The work included Portuguese and Spanish text and voice localisation.[3]

The adaptation’s credits identify the programmers and the Portuguese voice cast separately from id Software’s original team. Suzana Bueno voices the computers and Bruno Santos the radio; members of the programming team also voiced prisoners.[4]

Release and reception

Quake II’s early availability was uneven. UOL could not test it in June 2009 because, according to Tectoy’s press office, problems had caused its removal from the download servers.[5]

By March 2010, Daniel (Chrome) could review the game for Zeebo Club. He praised the Portuguese voice work and the full sequence of PC levels, while noting reduced lighting, missing introductory video and no multiplayer. He also recorded reports of downloads appearing on some consoles but not others, and crashes when new areas loaded.[6]

The console offered two control presets. Casual added automatic aiming; Expert used one analogue stick to move and the other to look. Aim sensitivity could be adjusted. Daniel found the Z-Pad’s small sticks took practice, but welcomed hearing Brazilian Portuguese radio chatter during the opening mission.[6]

Quake II was later removed from sale alongside Quake. The company’s June 2010 statement said existing downloaded copies could still be played, provided they had not been deleted.[7]
