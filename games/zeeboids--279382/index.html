The application

Zeeboids creates 3D characters with customizable appearances. Compatible games use those characters as players, including Foot Camp and Super League.[2][3]

At launch, players could synchronize their Zeeboids, skills and achievements through ZeeboNet and retrieve them on another console, such as a friend’s system.[4]

Development

Tiago Tex Pine led an eight-person team and handled the core product design. Nintendo’s Miis were an explicit inspiration. He worked with programmers to decide how much appearance customization the hardware could support while maintaining the frame rate.[3]

The application used the team’s internal OpenGL ES engine with C++, BREW and Adreno SDKs.[3]

Pine’s original intention was an avatar system native to the console, with Zeeboids initially free on the store. The characters would be reused in other games, rather than staying inside a standalone editor.[3]

Release and reception

Zeebo announced Zeeboids alongside Foot Camp in June 2010. Both were developed by Zeebo Interactive Studios in Brazil.[4]

Launch-era Brazilian reporting listed Zeeboids separately at 290 Z-Credits, alongside Foot Camp’s promotional price. That differs from the original free-distribution intention Pine recalls in his portfolio. The same report presented the avatars as a continuing part of later games in the football series.[5][3]
