The game

FIFA 09 on Zeebo offers four modes: a friendly match for one or two players, ten set challenges, a full season in one of eleven leagues, and a choice of ten tournaments. Players can change their lineup and roles, while a stamina meter limits continuous running.[2]

Development

The credits retain Gamelion’s team, led by Krzysztof Jędruczyk, with producers Hubert Borowy and Sebastian Szczygieł. The Brazilian adaptation team included producer Hugo Faria; programmers Edgar Vilela Gadbem, Henrique Mendes and Leonardo Loures Lopes; artists Carina Cheng, Seigi Sato and Suellen Salgado; and musician Denis Koishi.[2][3]

John Rizzo described FIFA as a hybrid: part of the game came from mobile content, while the voiceovers came from PC.[4]

That included Portuguese commentary by Nivaldo Prieto. The reproduced ZeeboBrasil review also identified Curumin’s recording “Magrela Fever” in the menus. The commentary and licensed music brought familiar elements from the larger FIFA releases into a much smaller game.[5]

Release and reception

FIFA 09 was installed on the first Brazilian retail consoles. Claudio Prandoni’s June 2009 UOL report criticised its slow response and rudimentary presentation while noting that the promised Portuguese commentary was present.[6]

The ZeeboBrasil review preserved by Zeebo Club was similarly mixed: licensed teams, Brazilian clubs and several competition modes were welcome, but player movement and defensive behaviour felt crude. The writer specifically noted that tackling was semi-automatic, with no dedicated tackle button.[5]
